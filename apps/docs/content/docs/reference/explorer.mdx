title: Explorer

The [Gyblos explorer](https://explorer.gyblos.com) shows the sellers, models, usage and payments of the
network. This page explains what its marks and amounts mean. The **?** next to a section title in the
explorer shows a short version of it.

## Pages [#pages]

| Page            | What it shows                                                                                                    |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Network (`/`)   | Recorded tokens and requests, sellers online per day, payments to sellers, current offers and recent operations. |
| `/sellers`      | Every seller online now, and the ones offline or not published.                                                  |
| `/models`       | Each model offered now, its lowest paid prices, and what was served over the range.                              |
| `/transactions` | Every Gyblos channel transaction, newest first.                                                                  |
| `/stats`        | Usage and payments over the range, with their daily tables and unserved demand.                                  |

All historical periods end at the last completed UTC calendar day. **All** opens the available history. **24h** opens the last completed UTC day, and **7d** and **30d** open that many completed days. Each chart date represents one UTC day. An observed zero and a day without observations have different marks. Daily payouts and settlements show a recorded amount whenever one is available. An empty day shows zero only when the chain index reports coverage for that entire UTC day.

On Network, select tokens, requests or seller payouts. The chart follows the page period unless you change its own selector. **Daily breakdown** opens that metric and period in Stats. The daily tokens table divides the total into input and output, the requests table into free and paid. Payments shows the amount received by sellers separately from settlements. Usage and Payments share the period selector. All-time totals compare the latest two closed seven-day windows when a baseline is available. Receipt comparisons show the number of observed days on each side.

Current offers have their own update time. Open the model, seller or wallet address from an offer row, or the individual operation from its transaction row. Tables keep their headers and pagination; on narrow screens the daily tables let you choose a column group or all columns.

Model and seller pages show published input and output prices in USDC per million tokens. Each bar is one indexed offer period. The selector switches between sellers or model variants and context sizes. Fixed ranges follow the selected UTC days. All covers up to 92 days of retained index history. A partial-history notice means that older matching periods may be missing.

A seller counts as online when the discovery index lists it. Column headers with arrows cycle through descending, ascending and default order. Dates and demand start with the newest days and highest demand respectively. For a fixed period, the comparison uses the immediately preceding period of equal length.

**Sellers online** on Network shows, for each UTC day, the average number of NodeIds with an accepted presence in each 5-minute slot. It has its own **7d**, **30d** and **90d** selector (30d by default) and does not follow the page period; its last bar is today, marked "so far". It is close to, but not the same as, the count of sellers online now, which also needs a current catalog. Hovering a day shows its peak and how many slots the index watched. "no index heartbeat" marks a day before the index recorded that it was running: its average counts slots with no recorded presence as zero, so it can read low, and such a day with no sellers at all is a gap, not a zero. A past day not summarized yet shows "pending". The headline averages the full days shown, without today.

In the requests-per-day charts on Stats, hovering a day shows how complete it is: "so far" for today, "pending" for a day the collector has not counted yet (today too, for up to 10 minutes after new requests; its bar may still show the earlier count), and "not captured" for a day before the collector started, which is not a zero. A day without a note is final, so an empty one is a real zero, unless the collector reported no state for it (an older collector), which also shows no note.

## Check mark and names [#verified]

A ✓ next to a seller means the discovery index checked that the named GitHub account published the
seller's NodeId in a gist. It links the seller to that account. It does not identify a person and says
nothing about the quality of the seller's answers. Sellers without it are not marked. Sellers set it up
with [Verify your GitHub account](/en/sellers/identity#verify-github).

A name in smaller type below an account or NodeId is a name the seller chose. Nobody verifies it, and
another seller can use the same name. The ✓ always belongs to the GitHub account, never to the name.

## Buyers [#buyers]

The Buyers card in Usage on Stats counts distinct buyers of all time: free buyers
by NodeId and paid buyers by wallet (or by NodeId when the receipt names no wallet), never added
together. For a 24h, 7-day or 30-day window, the card also shows newly seen buyers. A buyer counts from its first receipt reported by the seller and not disputed by the buyer, and stays counted even if that receipt is disputed later. Both are counted up to the end of yesterday (UTC), so today's new buyers appear
tomorrow. A count below 5, zero included, is shown as "< 5". See
[Data and privacy](/en/reference/data-and-privacy#receipts) for what these counts can and cannot hide.

## Channel transactions [#channel-transactions]

The page numbers let you jump directly to a page of operations and show how many pages match the current recipient filter.

Each row is one change to a Gyblos payment channel. The amount depends on the type:

| Type            | Amount                                                                                                |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Deposit, Top-up | What the buyer put into the channel.                                                                  |
| Settle          | What was newly settled.                                                                               |
| Paid out        | What the seller side received (payee and payout recipients). It is green, unless it is a batch total. |
| Refunded        | What went back to the buyer.                                                                          |

Next to a payout, its own columns show the rest of its split:

- **Gyblos fee**: the Gyblos protocol fee.
- **Program**: the payment program's own treasury, for rounding remainders or a share it could
  not deliver. It is not a Gyblos fee.
- **To buyer**: what went back to the buyer.

USDC that left the channel to no party the channel names has no column: a note under the amount says
how much is unclassified. When a refund row's transaction also paid sellers, a note under the refund says
how much went to sellers.

One transaction can pay out several channels. Its batch total is shown on its first row, and the other
rows point to that transaction. Don't add the amounts up: you would count the same USDC more than once.

## A seller's transactions [#seller-transactions]

A seller's page lists the channels whose payout recipient is the owner address the seller declared. The
seller chooses that address, so it is not verified. When another listed seller declares the same address,
the explorer says so, and the channels may belong to either seller. The amounts read as in
[Channel transactions](#channel-transactions).
